Work-Based Learning

Work-Based Learning (WBL) is a crucial component of Career and Technical Education (CTE) pathways that offers students real-world work experiences aligned with their chosen career fields. It provides them with the opportunity to apply classroom knowledge in practical settings, gain hands-on skills, and develop professional networks. WBL experiences are typically structured and supervised to ensure a valuable and safe learning environment. Here are some common forms of WBL within our CTE programs:

  • Internships: Internships involve students working in a professional setting, either during the school year or over the summer. They provide an immersive experience in a specific industry or occupation, allowing students to observe and participate in real work tasks under the guidance of experienced professionals. Internships are part-time, and they offer students a chance to learn about industry practices, gain exposure to workplace culture, and build their professional networks.
  • Apprenticeships: Apprenticeships are formal, structured programs that combine on-the-job training with classroom instruction. Students, known as apprentices, work under the supervision of skilled mentors, gaining progressively more responsibility and expertise over time. Apprenticeships offer a highly immersive and in-depth learning experience, leading to industry-recognized certifications or licenses and a solid foundation for long-term career success.
  • Job Shadowing: Job shadowing involves students observing and learning from professionals in their chosen field. During a job shadowing experience, students spend a day or a few hours shadowing a professional, observing their daily tasks, and gaining insights into the profession's requirements and challenges. Job shadowing helps students explore potential career paths, make informed decisions about their future, and understand the skills and qualifications needed for success in a particular industry.
